# SkitourSafe — PRD

## Vision & Problem Statement

**Pain:** A ski tourer with 1–3 seasons, planning an outing alone, the evening before departure (at home, laptop/phone), loses 1–2 hours on manually piecing together information: the avalanche danger level from the bulletin, wind and visibility from the forecast, slope angle and aspect of routes from the map, and comparing it all with their own experience and gear. When the analysis exceeds their competence, it ends in decision paralysis — choosing a route "by feel", going into terrain they cannot assess, or giving up.

**Insight:** Two reasons the status quo does not solve this. First, responsibility: app creators are afraid of an unambiguous message "this route is safe today", so they hand the user raw data with the message "assess it yourself". Second, integration: precisely linking GIS to avalanche risk per meter of path is technologically expensive. We solve both problems deliberately: we accept responsibility for the recommendation within the bounds of an explicit disclaimer (it is the recommendation of a filtering system, not an absolute truth), and we work on manually catalogued, popular routes as wholes, instead of automatic geometry analysis.

## User & Persona

**Primary persona:** A ski tourer with 1–3 seasons of experience. Travels under their own power, plans weekend outings alone or in a small group. Knows the basics of avalanche theory, but has neither the habit nor the time for a full topographical-avalanche analysis. Reaches for the product in the evening before an outing, at home, on a laptop or phone.

## Success Criteria

### Primary
- A logged-in user, in under 15 seconds from submitting the form, receives at least 1 accurate, safe route (selected by hard filters based on the entered conditions) together with a personalized justification generated by AI and the ability to save it to their outing history.

### Secondary
- Presentation of the recommended route on an interactive card with an expandable list of alternatives and an estimated traversal time computed from the fixed declaration of the fitness level in the user profile (beginner / intermediate / advanced), recalculated with simple time coefficients.

### Guardrails
- The AI layer never recommends a route that was rejected by a hard deterministic filter (e.g. missing ABC gear blocks routes requiring ABC, and avalanche level 4/5 entirely blocks a route).
- The correct, safe result of the hard filter is displayed to the user even when AI justification generation is unavailable (failure or no response).

## User Stories

### US-01: Planning an outing and receiving a safe recommendation

- **Given** a logged-in user (a ski tourer) is on the planning dashboard and has filled in the form with their skier profile and current conditions
- **When** they accept the liability disclaimer and click "Find a safe route"
- **Then** the system filters the route database and displays a safe recommendation with an AI-generated justification (with the option to save it to the outing journal), and in the case of extreme conditions displays a "NO-GO" warning stating that there are no safe options

#### Acceptance Criteria
- **Filter primacy:** no route rejected by deterministic rules may be proposed to the user by the AI model.
- **Fallback mechanism:** in case of an error or a timeout waiting for the justification to be generated, the app still displays the filtered, safe routes, replacing the AI justification with a static system message.
- **Empty state (NO-GO):** if the hard filters reject 100% of the routes in the database (e.g. at avalanche danger level 4 or 5), the AI request is skipped, and the user sees a clear, educational alert against any outings outside the ski area, explaining why going out is impossible.
- **The form remains available:** level 4–5 does not block filling in the form or starting the analysis — the user must be able to reach the NO-GO result and read its justification, instead of hitting a blocked screen.

## Functional Requirements

### Account & access
- FR-001: A user can create an account and log in with email and password. Priority: must-have
- FR-002: A user can log out and remain logged out on return. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counterargument considered: "Logging out before entering a no-coverage zone would cut the user off from a critical safety plan." Resolution: FR stays; explicit non-goal — the product serves home planning, not field navigation.

### Outing journal
- FR-003: A user can see their outing journal, empty at the first session. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counterargument considered: "Outing history may build a false sense of safety based on outdated data." Resolution: FR stays; each entry records the conditions from the day it was recorded and explicitly communicates their staleness as a recommendation.

### Planning form
- FR-004: A user can provide their skier profile — ABC gear, terrain familiarity, fitness level (beginner / intermediate / advanced), available time. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counterargument considered: "Terrain self-assessment tends to be inflated and leads to recommendations above one's ability." Resolution: FR stays; the filter applies a conservative interpretation of self-assessment.
- FR-005: A user can provide current conditions — avalanche level, strong-wind direction, visibility. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counterargument considered: "The whole filter depends on the correctness of the manual input." Resolution: FR stays; a confirmation screen appears before analysis (FR-010).
- FR-006: A user can confirm the liability disclaimer before analysis. Priority: must-have
- FR-010: A user can confirm the summary of the entered conditions and profile immediately before starting the analysis. Priority: must-have

### Analysis & recommendation
- FR-007: A user can start the analysis and see a recommended route with a justification and an alternative option; when no route passes the filters, the result is the NO-GO state. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counterargument considered: "A rigid implementation forcing always a main route and an alternative would try to generate the 'least bad' options instead of showing a categorical ban on going out." Resolution: FR changed — NO-GO is a valid outcome; the system never forces a recommendation.
- FR-008: A user can save an outing to the journal and return to it later. Priority: must-have
- FR-009: A user can see why the remaining routes were rejected. Priority: nice-to-have

## Non-Functional Requirements

- The user receives a ready route recommendation in under 15 seconds from submitting the form.
- In case textual justification generation is unavailable, the user still receives the selected list of safe routes with a default system message.
- Displaying any analysis result is impossible before the liability disclaimer has been explicitly accepted.
- The whole flow — from the form to the recommendation — remains legible and feasible on narrow, portrait phone screens.

## Business Logic

The app decides which ski-touring routes are safe on a given day, crossing current avalanche-weather conditions with the individual experience and equipment of the skier.

**Input:** the declared skier profile (possession of ABC rescue gear, experience level, available time) and the manually entered current conditions (avalanche level, wind direction, visibility).

**Result:** an unambiguous recommendation of a safe route with an understandable, textual justification of the decision (and a list of alternatives) OR a categorical "NO-GO" message against going into the mountains, if the conditions are extremely dangerous.

**How the user encounters it:** after confirming the conditions and the disclaimer the user sees the result as a recommendation card or a NO-GO alert; nothing between these two states is a valid outcome.

## Access Control

Login (email + password) — user account; profile and saved outings assigned to the account. Flat role model: every logged-in user sees and does the same. No administrative roles. A non-logged-in user has no access to the form or recommendations; they see the login screen.

**Account data & retention:** the only personal datum is the email address. Domain data — the skier profile (ABC gear, experience, fitness level) and the outing journal entries — are stored persistently, linked to the account, and removed together with the account when the user deletes it.

## Non-Goals

- **No integration with external weather-avalanche data sources** — rationale: conditions are entered manually; integration is the main external dependency that could stall the first flow.
- **No automatic GIS analysis of slope and aspect per meter** — rationale: routes are manually catalogued as wholes; automating the geometry is a cost outside the MVP scope.
- **No navigation, GPS, or offline mode** — rationale: the product plans in the evening at home and does not guide the user in the field.
- **No administrative roles and management panel** — rationale: the role model is flat, and routes enter the database as seed data.
- **No social features** — rationale: no sharing of outings, comments, or other users' profiles.
- **No full WCAG-AA compliance and no multilingualism** — rationale: an explicit non-functional non-goal; the MVP serves one person or a handful of users.
